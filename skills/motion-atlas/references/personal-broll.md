# Personal B-roll

Search the Personal B-roll catalog by occasion, action, and camera angle. These are specific ideas to film yourself doing ordinary actions; they are not stock footage or ready-made clips. Use the separate B-roll category for general footage subjects.

Start with the action and the story it supports. Pick a small set of shots: a wide view to establish the location, a medium view of the action, and a close detail. Use angle variants only when they contribute new information. Keep clothing, props, hand use, and action direction consistent between takes. Leave a moment before and after the action for editing.

For a requested shot list, describe camera placement, framing, subject movement, start frame, action, and end frame in concrete terms. Include specific variations such as side close-up opening a laptop, finger detail on a light switch, ground-level walking past, or stepping over a camera. Tailor actions to the user’s actual location and equipment. Do not claim a shot has been filmed simply because it appears in the catalog.

Use `templates/personal-broll-shot.json` for collected or planned shots. Keep reference material separate from the user’s own recorded footage. The attached outdoor filmmaking reference has a lightweight preview in the site and a link to the original Library video. It was reviewed through sampled frames; do not invent a transcript or identify the person as the user.

For physical educational explanations, search Tabletop paper explanations and Vsauce-inspired physical explainer coverage. Plan face-to-camera narration, overhead demonstrations, prop close-ups, reaction shots, and complete action coverage as separate shots. The named family is a requested collection direction, not an analysis of an imported creator video.
